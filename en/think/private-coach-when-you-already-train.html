COMPARE · ALREADY TRAINING

You can already hit. So why aren't you improving?

You've done months, or a year, of group classes. You go two or three times a week. And you still have the same low guard, the same kick that doesn't turn, the same mess in the clinch. It's not a lack of hours. It's that in a class of 20, nobody has time to watch yours. That's what a personal coach is for.

How it works alongside your gym What we fix first

I'm not here to replace your coach or your club. I'm here to do what doesn't fit in a group: watch you, for sixty minutes, and tell you the truth about what you're doing wrong.

WHAT I USUALLY SEE

The five flaws nobody corrects in a group class.

I've seen them in hundreds of gym students who arrived convinced they were advanced. It's not their fault or their coach's. It's the format.

The guard drops when you strike

When you throw the cross, the other hand comes down. On the bag nothing happens. In sparring you pay for it. Fixed in two sessions with corrected repetition on every strike.

The kick doesn't turn the hip

A leg kick, not a hip kick. Pretty on video, weak on the pad. The difference between a kick that hurts and one that doesn't is in the standing foot.

The clinch is a hug

You grab and wait. The clinch is head control, angles and off-balancing. It's the part least taught in groups and the one that changes your Muay Thai the most.

Fixed distance

Always the same distance from the opponent. Entering, striking, exiting on an angle: that's taught one-on-one, by someone pushing you out of your comfort zone.

Defence by reflex, not by reading

You block after you've been hit. Reading the shoulder, the foot, the weight shift. That takes repetitions with immediate feedback, not a coach on the other side of the room.

QUESTIONS

Questions from people who already train.

Do I need a personal coach if I already go to group classes?

Not automatically. First identify which skill and which feedback you're missing. If you've had the same flaw for six months, the answer is yes: nobody's going to correct it for you in a group of 20.

Isn't it disrespectful to my coach?

No. Any serious coach knows they can't watch everyone in a group. Many of my students come recommended by their own coach. If you'd rather we talk, I'm happy to.

How often?

One session a month already changes things if you practise what you take away. Every two weeks is the sweet spot for someone training in a group three times a week.

Do you work MMA and Jiu-Jitsu too?

MMA, yes: takedowns, takedown defence and some ground work, the part that works without a mat. Jiu-Jitsu, not right now.

Do you prepare people for competition?

Yes. Fight plan, strategy against a specific opponent, corner on fight day if it works out. We talk about it in the assessment.
