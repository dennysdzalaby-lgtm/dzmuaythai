Muay Thai for women. No audience.

The number one reason many women don't start isn't fear of getting hit. It's the thought of a gym full of men, a 90-kilo sparring partner and a coach who doesn't look at you. Here there's no audience, no partner, and the coach only looks at you.

What training with me is like Muay Thai and self-defence

The most direct option: a session where you're the only student. And if you want to come as a group of friends, that works too. I'm the one who teaches.

WHAT CHANGES FOR YOU

Four things my female students tell me they didn't expect.

Technique makes up for strength

A well-executed low kick doesn't depend on biceps. It depends on hips and timing. Women often learn this faster than men, because they don't try to muscle through it.

Pads are addictive

The sound of a clean strike on the pad is one of the most satisfying things there is. It's what makes the hour fly and makes you want to come back.

Nobody treats you like glass

I adapt the load, not the respect. You learn the same technique I teach a fighter, with the same corrections.

Confidence doesn't come from knowing how to hit

It comes from knowing where your feet are, how to keep distance, and that your body responds when you ask it to. You carry that off the mats.

About self-defence, since you're going to ask.

Many women come to Muay Thai wanting to be able to defend themselves. It's a legitimate reason and we work on it. But I won't tell you ten sessions make you invincible, because it isn't true and anyone who says so is selling you hot air.

What training does give you: distance, reaction, knowing where your body is, and a calm that shows. What needs adding: awareness of your surroundings, decisions, and knowing when to leave. I teach that too, and it has its own page.

QUESTIONS

What women ask before starting.

Do you have women-only classes?

Yes, if you come as a group: €45 a session for two; if there are more of you, message me and we'll work out the price. And if you'd rather train alone, it's one-on-one: you and me, nobody else.

Will I have to hit or be hit by someone?

No. We work technique and pads. There's no sparring unless you ask for it and I can see you're ready, and even then it's controlled.

I've never done a contact sport. Is that a problem?

It's the norm. Almost all my female students started from zero. The first session is adapted to where you are today.

Do I need to be strong to start?

No. Muay Thai technique lives in the hips and the timing, not the arms. My female students often learn that faster than men, because they don't try to muscle through it.

Can I come with a friend?

Yes. Training for two is available. It's a good way to start if doing it alone feels daunting.
